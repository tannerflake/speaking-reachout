# Outreach strategy memo: what worked July to October 2026

Source: 417 messages in cheryl@jeffflake.com since July 15, joined to the CRM tables. Raw mail stayed on this machine. Written October 11, 2026.

## The headline

The success came from one manual campaign, not from the app. Between September 28 and October 2 Jeff sent 87 emails himself from the cheryl@jeffflake.com inbox, signed as Cheryl under the subject "Campus Speaking Opportunity" (plus a handful of "November Conference" pitches to trade associations). Those 87 emails produced 20 human replies and 18 warm conversations in under two weeks.

The app's own July campaign sent 11 emails to think tanks and civic conference programs and got zero human replies.

| Campaign | Sent | Human replies | Warm | Hard no | Threads with a bounce |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fall manual blast (Sept 28 to Oct 2) | 87 | 20 (23%) | 18 | 4 | 23 (26%) |
| July app-sent (Jul 21 to Aug 12) | 11 | 1 (a decline) | 0 | 1 | 1 |

Who actually answers: the director of a named center, institute, or endowed lecture series. Heads of school and presidents forward the email to that person. Examples that replied within a day: the Athenaeum at CMC, the Riley Institute at Furman, the Culver Center at Simpson, the McCarthy Center at CSB/SJU, the Garfield Center at Hiram, the Clements Center at UT Austin, BIPP at Bucknell, the Center for Politics and the People at Ripon.

## Why the fall template worked and the app template did not

Side by side, the fall email does five things the July app email did not.

1. **Anchors to a specific season.** "During the 2027 winter/spring semester" or "as the Athenaeum plans its speakers for spring 2027 and the 2027–2028 academic year." The app wrote "as you look ahead to future conversations." A dated ask forces a yes, no, or "we are full," and all three are useful.
2. **Stacks peer social proof.** Exeter and Lawrenceville for prep schools. Stanford, Emory, Boston University, Michigan, Ohio State, Lake Forest for colleges. Ripon's reply opened with "I was talking with friends who really enjoyed Ambassador Flake's talk at Lake Forest." The list is the engine. The app email had no social proof.
3. **Sells the visit, not the speech.** Classroom discussions, lunch with students, dinner with school leadership, "those smaller, candid conversations are often the most meaningful part." Hosts replied asking about exactly that format.
4. **Names the money up front, softly.** "Travel expenses and a modest honorarium are customary" or "happy to discuss timing, format, and fee arrangements." The app's July rule set says not to mention fee flexibility, which is right, but the app emails said nothing about money at all. The fall emails also attached a one-page PDF of topics and prior engagements. The app sends no attachment.
5. **Plain subject lines.** "Campus Speaking Opportunity" and "November Conference." The app wrote "Jeff Flake for the Vanderbilt Project on Unity and American Democracy," which reads like a vendor pitch.

Two smaller things: the fall emails went to two or three people per institution (head plus center director plus an assistant), which is how the right person got it. And the nonpartisan line ("he does not engage in partisan commentary") shows up in every fall email and in none of the July ones.

## The second engine: inbound

Roughly half the inbound mail this period was unsolicited demand, and it is worth as much as the campaign.

- **Speaker bureaus.** Leading Authorities placed Jeff with the American Council of Engineering Companies at 20 to 25 thousand within 24 hours of first contact. Zero outreach cost.
- **Word of mouth between campuses.** Ripon heard about Lake Forest. St. George's is co-sponsoring a visit with the Pell Center at Salve Regina. Stanford's LDS student group asked for a side meeting once the Stanford talk was announced.
- **Media and podcasts.** BBC, Bloomberg, Boston Globe, New Yorker, Newsweek, Fox 5 Atlanta, CBC, Firstpost, The Good Fight, the Chuck Todd podcast. None of these pay, but they keep the name current, which is what makes the social-proof paragraph land.

## What is leaking

1. **One in four emails had a bounced address.** 23 of 87 threads contained at least one undeliverable or blocked recipient, mostly guessed addresses and departed staff. Several of the "no reply" institutions may simply never have received the email. Verify addresses before a blast, or send only to addresses found on the institution's site.
2. **No follow-up to the silent 67.** Every reply (Cheryl or Jeff handles these) was reactive and same-day, which is good. But nobody who stayed quiet got a second touch. Center directors are busy in October; a short nudge at the two to three week mark is normal and expected.
3. **Deferrals have no calendar.** At least 14 warm threads parked themselves on a date: Trinity in March, UF by November, CSB/SJU late October, Davidson and Deerfield for 2027-28, AICPA next year, and so on. None of that is written down anywhere the tool can see.
4. **The CRM did not know this campaign happened.** Fixed on October 11: all 87 fall targets are now leads, with their contacts, the sent email as an outreach row carrying the Gmail thread ID, reply and bounce dates, and the outcome as a note on each lead. Statuses: 16 interested, 5 not interested, 66 reached out. The 32 bounced addresses are marked as needing lookup. The CRM still shows zero bookings while Lake Forest, World Affairs Council of Atlanta, Emory, BU, Stanford, Michigan State, Exeter, Lawrenceville, Geneva, and ACEC all happened; those predate the campaign and were not imported.
5. **Threads get moved out of the connected mailbox.** Follow-up replies often say "I've added my primary email, cherylflake@gmail.com, to make communication easier." From then on the conversation lives in an inbox the tool is not connected to. Either connect that account instead, or stop switching.
6. **Prep school timing.** The spring 2027 prep school calendar was set by the time the emails went out. Prep schools should get the email in spring for the following year.

## Recommendations, in order

1. **Make the fall template the app's base template.** The concrete rules are listed below and have been added to the Tailoring tab.
2. **Send one follow-up now to the silent fall targets** (about 60 institutions after removing bounces). Two sentences: still planning spring and 2027-28, happy to send the one-pager, and a fresh social-proof line (Stanford on October 19, Michigan State, Emory, BU).
3. **Done: the fall campaign is in the CRM.** Deferral dates live in each lead's notes. The reply scanner can now match these threads, since the outreach rows carry the Gmail thread IDs.
4. **Target by budget signal.** Named centers, institutes, and endowed lecture series answered and can pay. Think tanks and civic conference programs did not answer. Generic "speaker series" inboxes forwarded to a form. Louisville and W&M show that state schools without a funded center will stall on fee.
5. **Run prep schools in February and March for the following academic year.** Colleges can be hit in September for spring and the next academic year.
6. **Ask every host for one introduction.** Each visit should produce a named peer contact. That is where Ripon and St. George's came from.
7. **Fix addresses before the next blast.** Check each recipient against the institution's directory page. The app's contact finder can do this for the leads once they are imported.

## Rules added to the Tailoring tab

- Anchor every cold ask to a specific semester or academic year, never "future" or "upcoming."
- Include one sentence of peer social proof naming recent comparable hosts. Prep schools: Exeter and Lawrenceville. Colleges: Stanford, Emory, Boston University, Michigan, Ohio State, Lake Forest, and others as they happen.
- Describe the visit as more than a talk: classroom discussion, a meal with students, dinner with leadership, and say the small conversations are the most meaningful part.
- Mention the attached one-page PDF of topics and prior engagements, and say travel and an honorarium are customary. No number in the first email.
- Subject lines are plain and descriptive: "Campus Speaking Opportunity" for schools, "[Month] Conference" for associations.
- State that Jeff is nonpartisan and that his presentations are student-focused and discussion-oriented.
- Targeting: prioritize named campus centers, institutes, and endowed lecture series; send to the director and the head's office; deprioritize think tanks and civic conference programs.
- Trade associations: open with what the election and the next Congress mean for the members' industry, acknowledge the program may already be set, and offer a luncheon, dinner, or leadership session as alternatives.
- Cadence: reply the same day, send one follow-up two to three weeks after silence, and when a host defers, close warmly and record the date to reconnect.
- Keep every conversation in the connected mailbox. Never add cherylflake@gmail.com to a thread.

Scenarios, reframed around commission capture (updated October 11)

Commission follows the first outreach to a lead, and only app-sent outreach counts. Jeff's own blasts (Aug 2025, Feb to Mar 2026, Jun 2026, Sep to Oct 2026, roughly three a year) claim every institution they touch. So the game is not a steady drip; it is reaching the untouched target universe through the app before Jeff's next blast, which the history says is February or March 2027.

All three plans claim the same prime pool; faster plans just finish before Jeff does and leave time to widen to prep schools (spring send), trade associations, and second-tier universities. The ceiling on commission is Jeff's calendar: 35 to 40 dates a year at 20,000 is 700,000 to 800,000 gross, so the commission pool is 140,000 to 160,000 a year. Today Tanner's share of that pool is zero because every booked date started in Gmail.

Prime target universe, rough count: about 600 colleges and universities with a named politics, public affairs, or international affairs center or an endowed lecture series; about 300 prep schools (send in February for the following year); trade associations in the thousands, lower hit rate but higher fees.

Two conditions. First, measure the first 50 app sends before scaling; the July app template got 0 of 11, and the fix is the rules added October 11, not yet proven. Second, contact lookups cost credits, so discovery should pull addresses from institution directory pages first and use the paid finder only for gaps.
